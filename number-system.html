foundations

The number system gives structure to all calculation.

Definition

A number system is a way of organizing numbers according to their properties and the operations we can perform on them. Different systems include natural numbers, integers, rational numbers, irrational numbers, and real numbers.

Natural numbers

The natural numbers are the counting numbers: 1, 2, 3, 4, ... They are the simplest numbers and the ones most frequently used for counting objects.

Integers

Integers include positive numbers, negative numbers, and zero. They extend the natural numbers to allow direction and magnitude in both positive and negative senses.

Rational numbers

Rational numbers are numbers that can be written as a fraction of two integers, such as 1/2, 3/4, or -5/7. They represent exact ratios and are central to arithmetic and measurement.

Irrational numbers

Irrational numbers cannot be expressed exactly as fractions of integers. Famous examples include √2 and π. Their decimal expansions continue forever without repeating.

Real numbers

Real numbers consist of both rational and irrational numbers. They correspond to every point on the number line and are fundamental to analysis and geometry.

Key idea

The number system is not just a list of categories. It shows how mathematical objects can be organized by structure, and how different kinds of numbers respond to the operations we perform on them.

Chapter synopses

  1. Chapter 1 — The Real Number System. Rational and irrational numbers, set and interval notation, indices, surds, and logarithms — the notation and algebraic vocabulary used throughout the book.
  2. Chapter 2 — Functions. Domains and ranges, common families, transformations, composite and piecewise functions, and inverses — the correspondence between algebraic rules and graphs.
  3. Chapter 3 — Polynomials. Arithmetic, division, factorisation, the remainder and factor theorems, and partial fractions (needed again in integral calculus).
  4. Chapter 4 — Inequalities. Quadratic, cubic, rational, and absolute-value inequalities.
  5. Chapter 5 — Matrices. Operations, determinants, inverses, and systems of simultaneous linear equations — foundations of linear algebra for graphics and machine learning.
  6. Chapter 6 — Trigonometry. Angle measure, the basic functions, graphs, identities, equations, and inverses.
  7. Chapter 7 — Linear Law. Turning non-linear relationships into straight-line form: scatter plots, best fit, gradients and intercepts in models.
  8. Chapter 8 — Coordinate Geometry and Conic Sections. Parabolas, ellipses, hyperbolas, and parametric equations.
  9. Chapter 9 — Probability and Statistics. Permutations and combinations; the binomial, normal, Poisson, and exponential distributions.
  10. Chapter 10 — Complex Numbers. Algebraic, polar, exponential, and geometric forms, with De Moivre's theorem and Euler's formula — numbers as points with geometric structure.
  11. Chapter 11 — Hyperbolic Functions. Identities, graphs, and inverses, alongside comparisons with the circular functions of Chapter 6.
  12. Chapter 12 — Differential Calculus. Limits and first principles, the standard rules, and applications to rates of change and optimisation.

Chapter synopses, continued

  1. Chapter 13 — Integral Calculus. Integration as the inverse of differentiation: methods, definite integrals, accumulated quantities and areas.
  2. Chapter 14 — Differential Equations. First-order equations and models of growth, decay, mixing, and continuous change.
  3. Chapter 15 — Sequences and Series. Convergence, binomial and power series, Taylor and Maclaurin expansions.
  4. Chapter 16 — Vectors. Two and three dimensions: operations, products, relative motion, vector-valued differentiation and integration.
  5. Chapter 17 — Introduction to Graph Theory. Vertices and edges, paths, cycles, trees, planar graphs, colouring, shortest paths, spanning trees — discrete maths for CS and networks.
  6. Chapter 18 — Introduction to Logical Reasoning. Propositions, truth tables, implications, quantifiers, valid arguments, fallacies, and elementary proof methods.
  7. Chapter 19 (bonus) — Computing with R. Vectors, data frames, importing and visualising data, probability distributions, one-sample tests, and matrix calculations — applying Chapters 5 and 9 computationally.
